🟢 INTRO

Slide 1 – Hook
“Have you ever met someone and instantly decided you didn’t like them?
Maybe it was the way they looked, spoke, or acted.
And just like that—your opinion was formed in seconds.”

Slide 2 – The Idea
“Our brains are incredibly fast.
They are constantly making quick decisions to help us understand the world.
But sometimes, these fast judgments are not accurate at all.”

Slide 3 – Thesis
“First impressions feel real and convincing.
But the truth is—they are often wrong.
And even worse, they can damage relationships before they even begin.”

🔵 MAIN PART

Slide 4 – Speed of Judgment
“Studies show that we form first impressions in just a few seconds.
Sometimes even less than a second.
That means we judge people before they even have a chance to speak.”

Slide 5 – Why We Do It
“This happens because our brain wants to save time and energy.
It uses shortcuts to quickly decide if someone is safe, friendly, or trustworthy.
But these shortcuts are not always reliable.”

Slide 6 – What We Judge
“We judge people based on appearance, clothing, posture, and tone of voice.
Even small details can influence how we see someone.
And most of this happens without us realizing it.”

Slide 7 – The Halo Effect
“If we notice one positive thing about a person,
we tend to assume everything about them is good.
This is called the halo effect.”

Slide 8 – The Horn Effect
“On the other hand,
if we notice one negative detail,
we may assume everything about that person is bad.
This is known as the horn effect.”

Slide 9 – Example (Shy Person)
“For example,
a quiet person might seem rude or uninterested.
But in reality, they could just be shy or nervous.”

Slide 10 – Example (Appearance)
“Or someone who dresses well might seem confident and capable.
But appearance does not always reflect personality or ability.”

Slide 11 – Confirmation Bias
“Once we form an opinion,
we start looking for evidence to support it.
This is called confirmation bias.
We ignore anything that proves us wrong.”

Slide 12 – Missed Opportunities
“As a result,
we may miss the chance to meet amazing people.
All because we judged them too quickly.”

Slide 13 – Damaged Relationships
“These quick judgments can also harm relationships.
They create distance, misunderstanding, and sometimes conflict.”

Slide 14 – Social Impact
“In groups,
first impressions can lead to exclusion.
People may be unfairly labeled or ignored.”

Slide 15 – Emotional Impact
“And for the person being judged,
it can be painful.
It affects their confidence and how they see themselves.”

Slide 16 – Reality Check
“The truth is,
people are complex.
No one can be fully understood in just a few seconds.”

Slide 17 – Change is Possible
“The good news is—we can change this.
We can become aware of our judgments
and choose to slow down.”

Slide 18 – What to Do
“Instead of judging quickly,
we can ask questions, listen carefully,
and give people time to show who they really are.”

🔴 CONCLUSION

Slide 19 – Key Message
“First impressions are fast,
but real understanding takes time.
And that time is worth it.”

Slide 20 – Final Impact
“So next time you meet someone,
pause before you judge.
Because one wrong first impression
could make you lose something truly valuable.”
